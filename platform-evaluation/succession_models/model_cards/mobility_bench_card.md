# Model card: internal mobility (Markov) and bench strength

> Generated by `run_all.py` on 2026-11-06 from the **synthetic** run. On synthetic data every number describes the synthetic generator, not the University.

## Intended use
Role-level career-structure analysis (feeder roles, dead ends, pathway diversity) and **bench strength** for critical role
families: expected internal-successor flow vs expected need over 24 months, with uncertainty. This is the talent-pool view;
it names no successor and ranks no person.

## Method
- Class-to-class transition counts from appointment histories over a 2016-10-31..2026-10-31
  window (2640 internal moves), symmetric Dirichlet prior, 95% Beta credible intervals per cell.
- Move and separation rates per class (Gamma posteriors); dead end = move rate < half the median and >= 70% of exits are
  separations; feeder = >= 50% of moves go up a level; effective pathways = exp(Shannon entropy of destinations).
- Bench: need = expected exits that leave the critical tier; supply = expected moves into it from feeder roles; both use the
  vacancy model's incumbent probabilities and the matrix; Monte Carlo over Dirichlet and Bernoulli noise.

## Limitations
- First-order Markov: ignores how long someone has been in role and their earlier path. Classes with few moves have wide intervals.
- Synthetic moves are push-driven (a person decides to move, a line is found or created); real internal moves are pulled by
  postings and civil-service registers, so supply is capacity, not guaranteed fills.
- Coverage ratios depend on the HR-owned critical-role list, which here is an assumption.
