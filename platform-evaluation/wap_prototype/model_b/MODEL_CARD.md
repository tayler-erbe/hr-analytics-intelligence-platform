With a same-campus peer set of 9-11 units, about 25% of units exceed the p75 by construction, so every method except B-ets sits at that base rate and B-ets passes at exactly the threshold on 10 flags. The selection therefore turns on 6/10 versus 3/10 flags and should be read as fragile; it is reported as the protocol requires, not re-run.

sMAPE on the count targets is dominated by zero quarters: a zero forecast against a zero actual scores 0 while any positive forecast against a zero actual scores 200, which is why B-snaive (often exactly zero) shows a far lower sMAPE than the smoother learners on hires and separations. MASE is the protocol's gating metric; sMAPE is reported for completeness.

## Limitations
- Small synthetic population (611-821 people): no dept x family series reaches mean headcount 10, so the dept grain the protocol names is empty here and the published grain is campus x job family. Annual campus x family is the realistic published grain on a population this size; the quarterly campus x family cells are publishable only because each modelled series has mean headcount >= 10.
- Short history (44 quarters, 24 scored origins) and small counts: quarterly separations per series average 0.5, retirements 0.01; B-snaive on such series is often zero and MASE scales are small, so relative MASE is noisy. Read the tables as 'does anything beat seasonal naive', not as precision.
- No COVID shock in the synthetic data; the COVID machinery is untested on real variation.
- Peer set for the attrition flag is the same campus (all modelled families) because the protocol's same-campus-same-family peer set is the unit itself at the pooled grain; this is a protocol gap to resolve in v2.
- Empirical intervals are pooled across series; per-series intervals need longer history.
- External covariates are one vintage (pseudo-out-of-sample) and QCEW ends 2024Q4, so 2025-2026 origins carry a forward-filled value.
- Hires counted from snapshot membership exclude people hired and separated inside one quarter; event-count hires are therefore higher.
